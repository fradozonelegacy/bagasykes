Method note
01 / Index construction

Health Burden Index

HBIᵢₜ = (1/n) Σ X*ₖᵢₜ

Normalize each index component to 0–1; reverse indicators for which higher values mean better access. The paper describes equal weighting but does not explicitly enumerate every HBI component in the formula section.

02 / Spatial lag

Neighbor-weighted HBI

(WHBI)ₜ = W × HBIₜ

W has dimensions 38 × 38; HBIₜ is a 38 × 1 vector, producing a 38 × 1 spatial lag. The weights need to be constructed from actual geography or connectivity.

03 / Candidate extension

Spatial Durbin Model

HBIₜ = ρWHBIₜ + Xₜβ + WXₜθ + μᵢ + τₜ + εᵢₜ

Candidate specification for direct and spillover effects, not estimated in the uploaded paper. Compare SAR/SEM/SDM diagnostics and report direct, indirect and total effects before drawing conclusions.

Uploaded paper baseline: pooled OLS, fixed effects and random effects; a one-year lagged robustness specification; Java vs outside-Java comparisons; diagnostic concerns include heteroskedasticity and overlap between health-facility and health-worker indicators.

What the paper reports

Paper summary
Association in baseline models

Healthcare facility availability, sanitation access and internet access are reported as negatively associated with HBI in the main specifications.

Regional heterogeneity

The paper reports different patterns between Java and provinces outside Java; poverty is more strongly related to HBI outside Java in the regional comparison.

Caution

The manuscript warns that findings are associations rather than definitive causal effects, and flags heteroskedasticity and high overlap between facility and health-worker indicators.

Source: Wicaksono & Pradana (2026), “Data-Driven Precision Health Policy in Indonesia: Uncovering Regional Health Needs through Socioeconomy, Healthcare Access, and Digital Determinants,” sections III–V.
